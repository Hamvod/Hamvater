# Hamvater — implementation plan

## Product scope
Hamvater is a virtual dress try-on experience. Users enter height and weight, select an adult male or female preview model, capture or upload a dress image, measure it with the linked `xcapt0/object_size_detector` engine where its assumptions are met, and inspect an animated avatar with the garment overlaid. The preview is exploratory only; it is not a fit guarantee or a cloth-physics simulation.

The linked engine detects a printed 5 × 5 cm ArUco marker (DICT_5X5_50), calibrates pixel scale, and estimates an object's outside contour width and height. Adapt it for captured/uploaded images, preserve attribution, and report missing-marker or invalid-contour cases rather than claiming calibrated measurements. The original male reference is [the user-supplied image](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcRgGW67A-bHilxNML7Q0rd1a4dc0c5Z_8WqZK-Q7X596g&s=10), a front-facing full-body 3D-style human figure with visible limb separation. The user-supplied [female human-body scene](https://www.mozaweb.com/Extra-3D_scenes-Human_body_female-209705) presents an interactive educational 3D figure and descriptive body layers. Use these as visual cues for full-body anatomy and inspectable 3D presentation; do not copy either source's image or model assets into the site.

## Implementation and serving
- **Client:** Flask serves semantic HTML/CSS/JavaScript. Pinned Three.js ES modules, GLTFLoader, and MeshoptDecoder are vendored locally so compressed GLB assets load without a runtime CDN dependency. The viewport loads original adult male/female GLB fitting models in modest neutral base garments, with model selection, pointer drag/turn controls, responsive lights, and a gentle idle animation. Height/weight update a clearly approximate viewport scale only; these two inputs do not reconstruct the body.
- **Garment overlay:** retain upload/camera, optional light-background cutout, marker-calibrated scan, direct placement/scale/opacity controls, before/after comparison, restart, and explicit scan confidence. Render the uploaded garment over the avatar in front view; hide the 2D overlay while inspecting a non-front model view. When calibrated contour bounds are available, crop the try-on layer to that garment region so the separate reference marker is not displayed as part of the garment; preserve the original scan photo for comparison.
- **Measurement API:** `POST /api/scan` accepts base64 image JSON, decodes with OpenCV, calls the adapted engine, and returns calibration status, estimated dimensions, garment contour bounds, and human-readable feedback. `GET /api/health` supports runtime checks. Image files are processed in memory and not persisted.
- **Production:** Python/Flask image, `requirements.txt`, and a Gunicorn Docker entrypoint honoring `$PORT` (default 3000). No login, durable profile storage, or database.
- **Project structure:** `server.py` owns routes and image decoding; `engine/object_size_detector.py` owns the attributed MIT detector adaptation; `static/index.html` owns the fitting workspace; `static/styles.css` owns minimal model-first layout and responsive sidebars; `static/app.js` owns profile, model, upload, scan, Three.js model loading, camera interaction, comparison state, and avatar scale; `static/models/` owns the two original generated GLBs; `static/vendor/three/` owns the pinned local renderer, loaders, Meshopt decoder, and license; `static/manus-routes.json` declares the page; `README.md`, `requirements.txt`, `Dockerfile`, and `app.config.ts` document and configure the app.

## Design — minimal model-first fitting workspace
- **Design movement:** quiet contemporary atelier interface with a restrained product-render stage. The human figure, not cards or decoration, is the visual centre.
- **Core principles:** lead with the model; keep controls in clearly separated sidebars; show calibration provenance honestly; reduce styling that competes with the garment.
- **Color philosophy:** warm near-white canvas grounds a realistic model without a busy backdrop. Ink teal `#183A47` carries labels and essential actions; muted terracotta `#D98F70` is a small selection/status accent; neutral gray hairlines divide control groups.
- **Layout paradigm:** a compact top bar and a three-part fitting workspace: profile/model rail at left, large isolated 3D stage in the middle, and garment/scan rail at right. No promotional hero block is inserted above the avatar. At mobile widths, keep the model first and stack both rails beneath it without covering or crowding the figure.
- **Signature elements:** a clean, almost-white fitting stage with a subtle ground shadow; restrained segmented controls for male/female and front/turn; compact calibration badges that stay outside the model silhouette.
- **Interaction philosophy:** let the avatar remain visible while people edit values; keep gender/model choices explicit; drag to inspect or turn; keep dress tools grouped away from the figure; preserve reversible undo/restart.
- **Animation:** gentle low-amplitude idle movement; direct pointer-driven rotation; brief dissolve only when swapping model assets. Respect reduced motion; no large motion or floating UI over the model.
- **Typography system:** DM Sans, 400–700, for all controls and labels; compact labels use legible letter spacing; measurements use tabular numerals. Do not add editorial headlines or decorative serif headings to the workspace.
- **Brand essence:** “An honest dress-preview workspace centred on selectable human forms and a garment scan.” Personality: clear, restrained, exploratory.
- **Brand voice:** short, factual, candid about approximation. Examples: “Choose a form to preview.” “Marker-calibrated estimate—not a fit promise.”
- **Wordmark & logo:** retain the compact bespoke ruler-tick “H” mark and Hamvater wordmark; do not let branding compete with the models.
- **Signature brand color:** ink teal `#183A47`.

## Key product boundary
The reference engine estimates an object's dimensions; it does not segment garments, scan bodies, recommend sizing, reconstruct body geometry, or simulate cloth. The generated male/female models are original fitting avatars, not medical references. Height and weight drive only a crude visualization. The overlaid garment remains a flat visual layer; no depth, fabric drape/stretch, complete individual body shape, tailoring, comfort, or real-world fit is represented.
